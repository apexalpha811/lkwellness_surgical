The problem

Wellness on the Westside is fragmented, and none of it is a place you want to stay.

A patient optimizing their health today visits a medspa for injectables, a recovery studio for cold plunge, a longevity clinic for labs, and a concierge doctor for hormones. Four intakes, four records, four bills, four waiting rooms. Not one of them is somewhere you would choose to spend a Saturday morning.

The insight

Others proved the format. Only a licensed facility can deliver it.

Remedy Place

Where a non-medical operator stops.

They proved the social wellness format works commercially, and they built it well. What they cannot do is medicine. No physician on site means no PRP, no IV therapy, no neuromodulators, no filler, no laser. Those are not features they chose to skip. They sit on the other side of a licensing and supervision threshold.

Soho House

Where the ladder was proven.

Their transferable asset is not exclusivity, it is the tiering. Soho Friends is a published annual tier that buys discounts and event invitations but no club access. What they do not have is medicine either.

The difference

L&K is not a practice built around a doctor. It is the facility a doctor needs.

The rooms, the diagnostics, the infusion suite, the operating suite, the staff and the accreditation, already standing. Physicians practice here. Investors own the building they practice in, which is why the surgical lines bill a facility fee while the surgeon bills their own patients directly. That is the leg up: not a head start on a format anyone can copy, but an asset a competitor has to become in order to compete.

The model

One building. Two layers. Different doors.

The clinic underneath is a licensed, cash-pay medical practice open to any member of the public at list price. The club on top is a capped, members-only social layer. Day passes let anyone taste the club without joining, and membershipMembership never becomes a condition of receiving care.

The offering

The surgery suite

One accredited operating room, inside the clinic.

A 900 square foot office-based surgery suite planned to the published iOR Partners specification: operating room, pre-op and PACU, sterile processing, sterile supply and soiled utility. Accredited by Quad A. It sits in the clinic layer, which means anyone can book it. Surgery is never a membership benefit.

What this suite collects

Facility fee only. The surgeon bills their own professional fee.

Every surgical figure in this brief is the facility fee — what L&K collects for the room, clinical staff, supplies, sedation monitoring and recovery. The operating surgeon is a principal who bills the patient directly for their professional work, and that fee never appears on these books. The all-in price a patient pays is materially higher than the numbers shown here.

This is why the operating-room margins run higher than the treatment-room lines: with no surgeon's share to pay, facility cost of goods is disposables, implants, drugs and linens only. Clinical staff already sits in fixed overhead. Across operating-room hours a month, the schedule works out to roughly per operating-room hour.

The ceiling, stated up front

Moderate sedation. Under two hours of recovery. Nothing above that line.

Every procedure runs under IV moderate sedation administered by the operating physician with a monitoring RN. That ceiling is a design decision, not a limitation we backed into. It is why there is no anesthesiologist on payroll, no anesthesia machine in the budget, and only three PACU bays — and it removes the highest-acuity risk from the building entirely.

Under California law, accreditation is mandatory only when anesthesia is dosed such that protective reflexes may be lost. Moderate sedation preserves them, so this suite most likely sits below the statutory trigger. We accredit anyway.

      Membership architecture

      A ladder, not a gate.

      Four tiers, each representing a meaningful step up rather than a repackaging of the last. The accessible annual tier serves as the entry point; the capped monthly tiers drive the core business. Mechanics follow a proven playbook: a three-month initial commitment followed by month-to-month billing, credits that roll over while membership remains active and expire upon cancellation, freezes with seven days’ notice, and tier changes available after the initial commitment.
